# Commercialization roadmap

This is a plan, not a list of shipped capabilities. Belweave operates openlaunch;
contact info@belweave.com. Hosted and self-hosted releases share the public
production repository. Existing first-party code is MIT. No enterprise-only
code or paid subscription is introduced by the pricing release.

## Pricing release

- Free is available with no plan-level device or agent cap and no core feature gates.
- Hosted storage, rate and concurrency protections remain fair-use dimensions.
- Team is planned and seat-based; do not publish a price before features and demand support it.
- Enterprise invites requirements discussions by email; no implied certification or SLA.
- Marketing stands alone; no external agent-provider partnership claims.
- Mailto links only. Demand tracking is deferred; PostHog may be evaluated later.
- Belweave maintains main for community functionality and the flagship hosted service.

## Before Team can launch

- [ ] Workspaces/organizations and owner, operator and viewer roles
- [ ] Usage visible to owners: active members excluding invites, actions, retention,
      active devices for fair-use and fleet quotes
- [ ] Billing provider integration, trial, cancellation and payment-state handling
- [ ] Pi 4 Model B and Uno R4 WiFi physical acceptance with recorded evidence
- [ ] First-party real-time wake acceptance on target hardware
- [ ] Approve-on-invoke notifications and a clearly bounded kill switch
- [ ] Billing and retention Terms/Privacy updates, with legal review
- [ ] Support process and realistic response commitments

Build priorities: approvals and kill switch; workspaces and roles; simulator/dry-run;
Home Assistant/MQTT/ESPHome bridges; real-time transport; groups, tags, health
alerts and OTA; audit export and SIEM integration. Existing grant expiry and
baseline history export must remain free. Paid value is collaboration, governance,
additional retention and support. No provider credentials belong on firmware.

## Billing and operational rules

Meter seats for Team, with unlimited devices within fair use. Enterprise may
quote fleet bands for high device-to-seat ratios. Consider retention, low-latency
connections and high action volume add-ons only when real costs justify them.
Validate cost drivers against hosting bills before setting prices. Review costs
30/60/90 days after launch; warn before throttling where practical.

Revocation, kill switch and retained audit/history reads must remain available
regardless of payment status. Never interrupt admitted work over billing. Add a
per-workspace usage ledger without coupling authorization or adapters to billing.

## Enterprise development

Develop through scoped design-partner pilots. Dedicated deployment, SSO/SAML,
SCIM, SIEM export, policy packs, DPA, SLA and compliance reporting require actual
implementation or agreed service commitments. Do not claim SOC 2 or any other
certification without evidence. Custom adapters may be paid services. Future
commercial additions must have an explicit separate license and boundary;
existing MIT rights remain intact. Do not create a separate production core.

## Owner and external follow-up

- [ ] Confirm shared-inbox support process; auto-reply/lead sheet if useful
- [ ] Legal review of hosted Terms, physical-device liability and fair use
- [ ] Review trademark policy and future commercial licensing needs
- [ ] Complete integration acceptance before publishing compatibility claims
- [ ] Complete physical acceptance on the owner's Mac; cloud tests are software only
- [ ] Plan OSS announcements after verification and documentation are ready
- [ ] Interview inbound leads before selecting Team price and trial terms

Dates are targets to set after acceptance, not public launch promises. Emailing
other teams, announcements, provisioning, OAuth grants, secrets and production
deployment require the applicable owner authorization.
